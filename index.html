With AChR-antibody MG

Immune antibodies target the receiving side. Receptors can be blocked or removed, and the surrounding surface can be damaged. The received signal is smaller, leaving less safety margin. Some muscle fibres may fail to respond, producing weakness.

The phone analogy: the sender still sends, but the receiving components are unreliable. A break may help for a while; it does not repair the immune problem.

Symptoms vary. This is an educational model of one common form of MG, not a test of health or muscle strength.

The signal, explained.

Myasthenia gravis (MG) causes weakness by disrupting communication between nerves and muscles. This game uses a phone comparison to introduce the process.

What the phone analogy represents

Phone storyIn the body
Sender phoneThe motor nerve ending
A message being sentAcetylcholine (ACh), a chemical messenger
Receiving componentsACh receptors and the muscle’s receiving surface
The receiver acts on the messageAn electrical muscle signal leads to contraction

MG is not a weak Wi-Fi signal, an empty battery, or a lack of effort. The chemical message does not literally contain words. The ACh message is converted into electrical activity in the muscle.

The full normal pathway
  1. An electrical impulse travels along a motor nerve.
  2. At the nerve ending, voltage-gated calcium channels open. Calcium enters and triggers vesicles to release ACh.
  3. ACh diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to nicotinic ACh receptors on the muscle.
  4. The receptor channels allow sodium in and potassium out. The net inward current creates an end-plate potential. If threshold is reached, voltage-gated sodium channels trigger a muscle action potential.
  5. The action potential travels along the muscle membrane and T-tubules, prompting calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Calcium acts through troponin and tropomyosin so actin and myosin can interact; ATP powers contraction.
  6. Acetylcholinesterase breaks down ACh, limiting further stimulation. After nerve stimulation stops, calcium is pumped back into storage and the muscle relaxes.

The steps are separated for learning. ACh breakdown is rapid and overlaps with muscle activation; it does not wait for contraction to finish.

What changes in MG

This model focuses on AChR-antibody MG. Autoantibodies can block receptor function, cross-link receptors so they are taken inside the cell and degraded, or activate complement proteins that damage the receiving membrane. These effects can overlap; they are not a fixed sequence.

Fewer effective receptors and membrane damage reduce the safety margin for transmission. During repeated activity, ACh release can initially decline. A smaller end-plate potential may then fail to trigger a muscle action potential. Across fibres, these failures reduce force.

Rest may allow temporary recovery of transmission, but does not reverse the underlying immune attack. Other MG forms, including MuSK- or LRP4-associated disease, have differences that this model does not cover.

Limits of the game

Each illustrated receiver represents many receptors. The three antibody effects are shown separately for learning, not in their real proportions. The response bars and threshold use invented values; they are not clinical measurements, percentages, or fixed predictions. A muscle action potential is all-or-none; the bar represents the signal that may trigger it.

The “move” message represents a simplified command. A real task such as lifting a cup needs many nerve impulses and many muscle fibres. Rest is not a battery recharge. The game does not imply that all exercise is harmful.

Symptoms & sources

Symptoms can include drooping eyelids, double vision, and weakness during chewing, swallowing or limb movement. Symptoms differ between people.

New or worsening breathing difficulty, or severe swallowing difficulty, needs urgent medical care.
